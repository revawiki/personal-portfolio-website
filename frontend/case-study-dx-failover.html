AWS Direct Connect Auto-Failover

Recurrent fiber-optic cuts kept taking down a hybrid link that a national telco's production workloads depended on. Every incident meant a human performing a manual switchover — at whatever hour it happened.

Context

The client runs Indonesia's largest telco cloud operation — production workloads spread across 20+ AWS accounts and 10+ Kubernetes clusters, reaching AWS over dedicated Direct Connect circuits rather than the public internet.

Dedicated circuits are physical. They run through real fiber, in real ground, alongside real construction work. Over the preceding period the link had been severed repeatedly — each cut an unplanned outage on a path that revenue-generating traffic depended on.

The problem

A redundant path existed. What did not exist was anything that moved traffic onto it without a person deciding to.

The switchover was a documented manual procedure: notice the incident, confirm it was physical rather than transient, then execute the cutover by hand. That sequence is slow at 2pm and slower at 2am, and every minute of it was billable downtime on a link carrying production traffic.

The failure mode was never "we don't have redundancy." It was "our redundancy needs a human to be awake."

Approach

I built an all-around automation for the failover path: detect the physical-level incident, validate that it is genuinely a link failure rather than a blip worth riding out, and execute the switchover to the redundant path without waiting on a person.

  1. Detect

    Continuously watch the Direct Connect link's health rather than waiting for a downstream service alarm to surface it second-hand.

  2. Validate

    Distinguish a real physical-layer failure from transient noise, so the system doesn't flap traffic on every momentary blip.

  3. Switch

    Execute the cutover to the redundant path automatically — the same steps the runbook prescribed, minus the wait for a human to run them.

  4. Notify

    Tell the operations team what happened and what state the link is now in, so the human involvement moves from executing to reviewing.

The generalized approach is published as an open-source repo, separate from any client-specific configuration.

Outcome

The manual switchover procedure was eliminated. Failover stopped depending on who was on shift and how quickly they could confirm what had happened.

Measured against the recurrent fiber-optic outage pattern that prompted the work, the automation accounts for IDR 79B+ in yearly revenue loss prevented — the difference between an outage that ends when a human reacts and one that ends when a system does.

It also became one of the initiatives cited in the client-acknowledged AI enablement work that led to an Innovator of the Month award in June 2025.

Lessons

  • Redundancy that needs a human isn't redundancy yet. It's a documented recovery plan. The gap between those two is measured in revenue.
  • Validate before you act. An automation that flaps on every transient blip creates a worse problem than the one it solves.
  • Keep the human in the loop, not in the path. The team still reviews every failover — they just no longer have to perform it.

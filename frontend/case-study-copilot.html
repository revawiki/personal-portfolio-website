Unified AI Operations Ecosystem

Cloud operations for a national telco meant humans watching dashboards, reading runbooks, and hand-checking change procedures across 20+ AWS accounts. The work was necessary. Almost none of it needed to be manual.

Context

Tech Mahindra runs cloud operations for Indonesia's largest telco. The estate spans 20+ AWS accounts and 10+ Kubernetes clusters carrying production workloads that serve millions of subscribers, under SLAs that do not bend.

I was brought in to direct AIOps R&D — leading a team of 4 engineers with a mandate to streamline day-to-day operations through AI and automation, so SLA attainment stops depending on how many experienced people happen to be awake.

The problem

Three distinct bottlenecks, each absorbed manually by the ops team:

  • Detection lag. Infrastructure anomalies surfaced through downstream symptoms — a service alarm, a customer report — rather than at the source. Mean time to detect sat around 20 minutes.
  • Triage lag. Once detected, first-level root cause analysis meant an engineer correlating monitoring, CMDB, and documentation by hand. Mean time to resolve sat around 30 minutes.
  • Change-review lag. Every Method-of-Procedure was reviewed manually before deployment, ~60 minutes per cycle, capping the team at 1–2 change requests per day.

None of these were knowledge problems. The team knew exactly what to do. They were throughput problems — the knowledge lived in people's heads and documents, and had to be retrieved by hand every time.

Automatic anomaly detection

The first build watched infrastructure signals directly instead of waiting for downstream symptoms to appear. Anomalies get surfaced at the source, with the context needed to act on them attached.

MTTD dropped from ~20 minutes to ≤5. That alone changed the shape of incidents — a problem caught in five minutes is frequently still a problem contained to one component.

AI-assisted first-level RCA

The next iteration added AI-assisted first-level root cause analysis on top of detection. Rather than an engineer manually correlating monitoring data, CMDB records, and runbook documentation, the system performs that correlation and presents a first-pass hypothesis.

MTTR dropped from ~30 minutes to ≤10. The engineer still makes the call — the system does the retrieval and the correlation that used to eat the first twenty minutes of every incident.

Automatic MOP checker

Separately, I introduced an automatic Method-of-Procedure checker that flags invalid steps before deployment rather than surfacing them during a review meeting — or worse, during execution.

~60 → ~20 min review cycle time
1–2 → 4+ change requests per day

Roughly tripling change throughput without adding headcount, and without removing the review — just removing the parts of it a machine can do more reliably than a tired human at the end of a shift.

Architecture

The individual wins were useful. Making them compound required treating them as one ecosystem rather than three tools, so I architected a unified AI operations layer with three pillars:

  1. RAG knowledge base

    Runbooks, documentation, CMDB and historical incidents made retrievable, so every agent reasons over the same operational ground truth instead of a hardcoded subset.

  2. MCP servers

    Tool access exposed through Model Context Protocol, so capabilities are declared once and reused across workflows rather than re-integrated per use case.

  3. Human-in-the-loop governance

    An explicit approval gate before anything touches production. The system analyzes and proposes; a human decides. That boundary is the design, not a limitation of it.

The result is a scalable foundation rather than a set of point solutions — new CloudOps AI capability plugs into existing retrieval, tooling, and governance instead of rebuilding all three.

Outcome

Detection 4x faster, resolution 3x faster, change throughput roughly tripled — and a foundation the team continues to build on rather than around.

Recognized as Innovator of the Month by Tech Mahindra's Chief People Officer in June 2025, driven by client-acknowledged AI enablement initiatives in cloud operations.

Lessons

  • Detection and resolution are separate problems. Solving MTTD did nothing for MTTR — they needed different builds, shipped in sequence.
  • Retrieval beats fine-tuning for ops knowledge. Runbooks change weekly. A RAG layer tracks that; a trained-in snapshot doesn't.
  • The approval gate is a feature. Nobody signs off on autonomous production changes in a telco. Designing for human approval from day one is what made adoption possible.
  • Throughput wins are quieter than outage wins, and often bigger. Tripling change capacity compounds every single day, not just during incidents.

Multi-Tenant ECS Platform Migration

The product had grown to 100+ monolithic EC2 deployments — roughly one per client. Every new customer meant new infrastructure, and the ceiling was getting closer with each signature.

Context

Netmonk is Telkom Indonesia's network monitoring product. It had grown the way successful products often do — one client at a time, each getting their own deployment because that was the fastest thing to do at the time.

By the time I was promoted to lead the DevOps initiative, that pattern had produced 100+ monolithic EC2 deployments. I led a team of 4 engineers to re-architect it.

The problem

One instance per client is a decision that charges compound interest. Every client carries their own idle capacity. Every patch is 100+ operations. Every new signature provisions new infrastructure before it generates any margin.

The architecture wasn't wrong when it was chosen. It became wrong at about client number thirty, and nobody had a reason to stop and say so.

Read: the hidden cost of one-instance-per-client →

Why ECS, not Kubernetes

The default answer to "we need container orchestration" is Kubernetes. We chose AWS ECS deliberately.

Kubernetes would have given us more capability than the workload needed, in exchange for an operational surface a 4-person team would then own permanently — control plane, upgrades, and a much steeper on-call learning curve for everyone who touched it afterwards.

ECS gave us what the migration actually required: client workloads decoupled from dedicated instances onto shared infrastructure, with an operational model the team could run without becoming Kubernetes specialists first.

The region move

The platform had been running in AWS Singapore because, when it was built, that was the nearest region. AWS Jakarta had since opened.

Rather than lift-and-shift, I re-architected the migration — combining the region move with the multi-tenancy transition so the estate was rebuilt correctly once rather than moved twice. That combined re-architecture is what produced the ~70% AWS infrastructure cost reduction.

Pipeline

Shared infrastructure raises the stakes on deployment: a bad release now reaches every tenant rather than one. The Bitbucket CI/CD pipeline was built with that in mind, with DevSecOps scanning integrated into the pipeline rather than bolted on after.

Deployment time dropped from ~10 minutes to ~5 — and, more usefully, became a repeatable process rather than a per-client procedure.

Outcome

~70% lower infrastructure cost, deploys twice as fast, and — the point of the whole exercise — no more per-client instance ceiling. Onboarding a new client stopped being an infrastructure event.

Lessons

  • Pick the orchestrator your team can run at 3am. ECS over Kubernetes cost us capability we didn't need and saved us operational load we'd have carried forever.
  • Combine migrations when they touch the same surface. Doing the region move and the multi-tenancy rebuild as one re-architecture is where the 70% came from.
  • Shared infrastructure raises the cost of a bad deploy. The pipeline and its security scanning weren't optional extras — they were the precondition.
  • Architectures expire quietly. One-instance-per-client never announced that it had stopped working. Someone has to go looking.
